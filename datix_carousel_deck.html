The Flaw in Individual Blame
Slide 2 of 7
⚠ The Punitive Reflex (Person-Centred)
Default Question
"Who made the omission, and why wasn't policy followed?"
Prescribed "Fix"
Reflective statements, mandatory retraining, and informal reprimand.
Ward Consequence
Under-reporting near misses due to fear; latent errors recur within months.
✓ The Systems Reality (Reason Model)
Systems-Level Question
"What latent vulnerabilities allowed this defense failure to breach the ward?"
Contributory Causes
Staffing spikes, cognitive overload, alert fatigue, and delayed cannulation access.
Governance Consequence
Psychological safety increases; engineered workflows prevent recurrence.

The 5-Stage Datix Incident Resolution Pathway
Slide 4 of 7
Stage 1
Incident Logging
Clinician submits objective factual timeline and immediate containment actions within 12 hours.
Stage 2
Triage & Risk Matrix
Governance team reviews via 5×5 matrix; initiates Duty of Candour (Regulation 20) if moderate harm.
Stage 3
Systems Review
Multidisciplinary Swarm Huddles or After Action Reviews evaluate environmental contributors.
Stage 4
SMART Action Fix
Hierarchy of Controls applied; physical & digital safeguards prioritized with accountable leads.
Stage 5
Closed-Loop Audit
90-day clinical verification audit, reporter feedback delivered, and national LFPSE sync.

Applied Clinical Deconstruction
Case Study: 4-Hour IV Sepsis Antibiotic Delay
Slide 5 of 7
The Clinical Scenario:
A post-operative patient developed a systemic fever of 38.9°C. Intravenous broad-spectrum antibiotics prescribed stat were administered 4 hours late. Traditional investigations would attribute fault to the bedside nurse.
Latent Contributory Factors (YCFF)
  • Workload Shock: Ward at 104% capacity with 2 acute admissions.
  • Consumable Depletion: Dedicated cannula packs missing from clean utility.
  • Alert Saturation: ePMA generated 14 non-urgent alarms in the prior hour.
Engineered Corrective Interventions
  • Pharmacy Restock: Visual Kanban 2-bin replenishment established.
  • Hard Forcing Stop: ePMA locks out routine documentation until stat doses clear.
  • Auto-Escalation: Automatic 60-min bleep alert triggered to Critical Care Outreach.

Action Design: The Hierarchy of Controls
Slide 6 of 7
1. Forcing Functions
Hard digital stops: ePMA prevents routine medication signoff until pending stat antibiotic doses are administered.
Strongest Defense
2. Physical Automation
Automated barcode bedside scanning and smart infusion pump dose error reduction systems.
High Reliability
3. Visual Management
Acute ward electronic bedboards with automated countdown sepsis clocks visible to full multi-disciplinary team.
Moderate Defense
4. Administrative Policy
Standard Operating Procedure updates, nursing handover checklists, and dual signoff forms.
Vulnerable
5. Re-education
Mandatory refresher e-learning on medication timing (Zero defense against systemic acute ward overload).
Ineffective Alone
